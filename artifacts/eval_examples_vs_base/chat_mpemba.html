Explain why warm water sometimes freezes faster than cold water.
Warm water can freeze faster than cold water due to the phenomenon known as "supercooling." When you add heat, it causes molecules in a liquid (like water) to move more quickly. In some cases, if this process happens too rapidly or unevenly, certain parts of the liquid may become supercooled – meaning they are below their freezing point but remain liquid because no ice crystals have formed yet.

When these supercooled areas come into contact with something cold like an ice surface, tiny bits of solid material from that object act as nucleation sites where crystal growth begins immediately despite being well below normal freezing temperatures for pure water (-4°C).
